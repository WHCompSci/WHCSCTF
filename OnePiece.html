How to Play

From unboxing your first pack to mastering tournament play — your complete guide.

🎯 Objective

Reduce your opponent's Life Points to zero. Each player starts with a hand of 5 cards and a Life area of 5 cards (face-down). When your Leader is attacked and loses the battle, the top Life card enters your hand. Once your Life area is empty and your Leader loses a battle — you lose.

🃏 Deck Rules

A legal deck contains exactly 50 cards: 1 Leader card + 49 Character/Event/Stage cards. Up to 4 copies of any card with the same card number are allowed. DON!! cards (10 total) are kept separate from your main deck.

01

Setup & Life Phase

Each player places their Leader card face-up in the Leader Area. Shuffle your 50-card deck and place 5 cards face-down as your Life cards. Draw 5 cards for your opening hand. Each player places all 10 DON!! cards in their DON!! Deck. Decide who goes first — the starting player cannot attack on turn 1.

02

Refresh Phase

At the start of your turn, un-rest (stand) all your rested cards including Characters and DON!! cards. Then reveal the top 1 card of your DON!! Deck and add it to your DON!! Area (this increases each turn until your 10 DON!! cards are all in play).

03

Draw Phase

Draw 1 card from the top of your deck. The starting player skips the Draw Phase on their very first turn only. If you cannot draw a card because your deck is empty, you lose the game.

04

DON!! Phase & Main Phase

Attach DON!! cards from your DON!! Area to your Leader or Characters to boost their Power by +1000 per DON!! attached. During Main Phase, play Character cards by paying their cost in DON!! (rest that many DON!! cards), activate card effects, and play Event cards. Stages are placed into the field and remain until replaced.

05

Attack Phase

You may attack with your Leader and any un-rested Characters. Declare a target (opponent's Leader or a Character). Your opponent may choose to block with a Blocker Character. Both sides reveal their Power; the higher Power wins. On a tie, the attacker wins. The loser is K.O.'d (sent to trash). If the target is the Leader and the attacker wins, one of the defender's Life cards goes to their hand.

06

Counter & Trigger Effects

During your opponent's attack, you may play Event cards with [Counter] from your hand — these grant your defending card +2000 or more Power. Some cards have a [Trigger] ability that activates when they enter your hand from the Life Area. Mastering counters and triggers is the hallmark of elite play.

Best Metas

Current competitive tier list — updated after the May 2026 Regional Championship results.

S Tier — Dominant

Red Luffy Gear 5

OP-13 Leader. Explosive DON!! burst. Wins through overwhelming Rush chains. ~32% top-cut share.

Purple Enel Control

OP-11 Leader. Hand disruption + life manipulation. Near-unbeatable endgame. Punishing for new players.

Green Law Swap

OP-03 Leader. Swap engine generates insurmountable tempo. Difficult skill floor but rewarding ceiling.

A Tier — Strong

Yellow Roger

OP-09 Leader. Life manipulation punishes aggro. Strong mirror matchup tools.

Blue Nami Stall

OP-08 Leader. Draw-pass loops with counter-heavy builds. Strong in Bo3.

Red Ace Burn

OP-04 Leader. Fast clock with burn damage via Event stacking. Weak to Blockers.

Black Sakazuki

OP-06 Leader. Removal-heavy. Strongest K.O. density in format. Slower clock.

B Tier — Viable

Purple Katakuri

Banned in Standard; still legal in Classic. Board-flood with DON!! acceleration.

Red/Green Yamato

Hybrid color consistency. Mid-range toolkit covers most matchups without excelling in any.

Blue Crocodile

Anti-meta tech. Punishes mono-Red hard. Struggles against Purple.

Yellow Sengoku

Marine tribal synergy. Snowballs if opponent cannot remove early board.

C Tier — Rogue / Off-Meta

Red Zoro Aggro

All-in beatdown. No recovery. Loses to any Blocker-heavy list.

Green Usopp Combo

Janky combo finish. Fun in casual; fragile to disruption.

Yellow Whitebeard

10-cost mana curve. Theoretically powerful. Practically too slow in current meta.

Eiichiro Oda

The visionary creator of One Piece — and the man whose world inspired an entire card game universe.

🖊️

Eiichiro Oda

尾田 栄一郎 — Oda Eiichirō

Born January 1, 1975 in Kumamoto, Japan, Eiichiro Oda is one of the best-selling manga authors in history. He debuted in Weekly Shōnen Jump in 1997 with One Piece, a pirate adventure that would grow into a cultural phenomenon spanning manga, anime, film, merchandise, games, and trading cards.

Biography

Oda developed a passion for drawing at an early age, citing manga artists Akira Toriyama (Dragon Ball) and Mitsutoshi Shimabukuro as early inspirations. At just 17 years old, he submitted his pirate-themed one-shot Wanted! to the Tezuka Award, finishing in second place and catching the attention of editors at Shueisha.

He went on to assist legendary mangaka Nobuhiro Watsuki (Rurouni Kenshin) before launching One Piece in Weekly Shōnen Jump on July 22, 1997. The series was an immediate success, regularly topping sales charts and establishing Oda as a generational talent.

Oda is renowned for his meticulous world-building, embedding foreshadowing across decades of storytelling. He works notoriously long hours — often sleeping only three hours a night — and is deeply protective of the One Piece narrative, personally reviewing and approving major adaptations including the One Piece TCG card illustrations and set storylines.

Oda's Influence on the Card Game

Unlike many IP-based card games, Bandai has maintained an unusually close relationship with Oda throughout the OPCG's development. Oda and his studio review all card illustrations to ensure consistency with canon — particularly for characters whose in-universe power levels and Devil Fruit abilities inform card mechanics.

Several cards in the game feature Oda's original sketches rendered by the card art team, most notably the OP-09 Gol D. Roger Secret Rare and the OP-04 Edward Newgate (Whitebeard) Leader, both considered crown jewels of any collection.

In an interview for Jump Festa 2024, Oda expressed surprise and delight at the depth of the card game's competitive scene: "I never imagined Luffy would one day be the reason people wake up at 5 AM to travel to a tournament. It moves me."
